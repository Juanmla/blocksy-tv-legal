Privacy Policy — Blocksy TV

Last updated: 1 October 2026

Short version: Blocksy TV keeps your usage data on your device. It has no user accounts, no servers of its own, no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party trackers. It does not sell or share your personal information.

The app connects to the internet for two purposes only: to check whether a newer version of the app is available, and, if the app crashes, to send a technical crash report so the problem can be fixed. Neither contains your usage history, your list of installed or blocked apps, your PIN, or anything on your screen.

Blocksy TV is an Android TV app that lets you block distracting apps and set daily time limits on others. This policy explains what the app accesses on your device, why, and where that information is kept.

Blocksy TV is published by an independent developer (wildasado.dev@gmail.com). References to “we” or “the app” mean Blocksy TV.

1. What the app accesses on your device, and why

Usage Access (PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS)

Android’s Usage Access permission lets Blocksy TV read how long each app has been in the foreground. The app uses this to:

  • enforce the daily time limits you set on specific apps;
  • show you the usage report (per-app screen time by day, week, month, and year).

This information is read directly from the operating system on your device and is only ever processed on your device.

Accessibility Service

Blocksy TV registers an Accessibility Service so it can tell which app has just come to the foreground. When you open an app you have blocked, or one that has passed its daily limit, the service triggers Blocksy’s full-screen block message.

The service is deliberately narrow. It reacts only to “window state changed” events and reads only the package name of the app in front (for example com.example.videoapp). It does not read the contents of the screen, text you type, form fields, passwords, messages, or any other on-screen information, and it does not record or transmit anything about your activity. The only thing it writes is a local count of how many times a block was triggered, used for the “blocked N×” figure in the report.

Installed apps list (QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES)

To let you choose which apps to block or limit, Blocksy TV reads the list of apps installed on your device — their names and icons. This list is shown to you in the app and is not stored or sent anywhere.

2. What is stored, and where

All of the following is kept in Blocksy TV’s private storage on your device. None of it is uploaded, backed up to a remote server by the app, or shared with anyone — including in crash reports (see section 4).

DataPurposeWhere
Daily per-app foreground time Usage report and time-limit enforcement Local database on device
Count of times a block was shown, per app “Blocked N×” figure in the report Local database on device
Your list of blocked apps and time limits The core blocking feature Local app settings on device
Settings PIN Protecting the settings screen from changes Stored only as a salted SHA-256 hash in Android’s Keystore-backed secure storage. The PIN itself is never stored.

Because there is no account, this data exists only on the device where you installed the app.

3. Data sharing and selling

None. Blocksy TV contains no advertising SDKs, no analytics SDKs, and no third-party trackers, and it never sells data. The only data that leaves your device is the technical information described in section 4, which is sent to service providers that process it on our behalf solely to deliver app updates and diagnose crashes — not for advertising, profiling, or any other purpose.

4. Network use

Blocksy TV works fully offline. It makes two kinds of network request.

Update check

It asks Expo’s update service (u.expo.dev) whether a newer JavaScript bundle for the app is available, so bug fixes can be delivered without a full app update. That request carries only technical information — the app version, the platform, the update channel, and a random installation identifier generated by the update library — and no personal data. Expo’s handling of that request is covered by Expo’s privacy policy.

Crash reports

If the app crashes or hits an unexpected error, it sends a crash report to Sentry, a crash-reporting service, so the bug can be found and fixed. A crash report contains:

  • the technical error and the location in the app’s code where it happened (a stack trace);
  • device and app diagnostics at the time of the crash — device model, Android version, app version, language and time-zone settings, and memory and storage state;
  • the Blocksy screen that was open and the in-app steps leading up to the error;
  • a random identifier generated when the app is installed, used only to count how many installations are affected by a crash. It is not a hardware or advertising identifier and is reset if you reinstall the app.

Crash reports are configured not to include your IP address, screenshots, the layout or contents of the screen, the list of apps installed on your device, your usage history, your blocked apps or limits, or your PIN. The one exception: if an error happens while Blocksy is handling a particular app, the technical error message may name that app’s package (for example com.example.videoapp). Crash reports are not linked to your identity or used to track you.

Crash reports are sent over an encrypted connection, processed by Sentry in the United States, and automatically deleted after at most 90 days. Sentry’s handling of this data is covered by Sentry’s privacy policy.

5. Children

Blocksy TV is a productivity and screen-time tool intended for the person who sets up the device. It does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. The only data that leaves the device is the technical information described in section 4, which does not identify the person using the TV.

6. Your controls and deleting your data

  • You can change or remove blocked apps, time limits, and the settings PIN at any time inside the app.
  • To erase everything Blocksy TV has stored: uninstall the app, or go to Android Settings → Apps → Blocksy TV → Storage → Clear data. This permanently deletes the local database, your settings, and the PIN hash. There is no account to delete.
  • Revoking the Usage Access or Accessibility permission in Android Settings immediately stops the app from reading that information.
  • Crash reports are deleted automatically (see section 4). To ask for them to be deleted sooner, email the address in section 8. Because reports are not linked to your identity, include roughly when the crash happened and your app version.

7. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the “last updated” date above will change, and any material change will be noted in the app’s release notes. Continued use of the app after an update means you accept the revised policy.
